# CORE GAMEPLAY

## Match Loop

Pitch selection -> pitch execution -> batting decision -> contact outcome -> fielding/read -> throw/cutoff -> runner decision -> tag/collision -> scoring/out -> rapid reset.

## Controls Philosophy

Few verbs, deep timing. Every core action should be understandable on a controller and keyboard. Context-sensitive actions are preferable to large ability bars.

## Pitching

Initial pitch families: Fastball, Breaking Ball, Change/Off-speed, Special/Signature. Aim + timing/accuracy determines execution. Stamina and confidence alter windows, not arbitrary dice rolls.

## Batting

Initial choices: Contact, Power, Bunt, Show/Trick modifier. Timing and plate location matter. Power increases reward and miss risk. Contact favors placement and consistency.

## Fielding

Prioritize readable routes, responsive switching, strong catch/throw feedback, cutoffs, dives, wall interactions, and quick relay decisions. Do not automate the fun out of defense.

## Base Running

Advance/hold/return, steal, slide, and context-sensitive collision/trick action. Multiple runners need predictable controls and clear indicators.

## Physicality

Contact is situational rather than constant. At high-value tag situations, runner and defender may choose normal baseball or a risky physical option. Outcomes consider timing, momentum, Toughness, positioning, ball security, and relevant traits. Failed aggression must have meaningful downside.

## Show Plays

A Show modifier converts eligible ordinary actions into harder, more theatrical variants: no-look throw, glove flip, barehand catch, exaggerated wall play, trick delivery, etc. The player knowingly trades reliability for Show gain, personality expression, and possible momentum/fame benefits.

## Match Formats

Prototype: 6 innings for pace. Full game and alternate league formats can follow after the core loop is proven. Avoid committing to copied Banana Ball rules; create original rules inspired by pace and spectacle.

## Abilities

Abilities should alter opportunities, timing windows, movement, or situational options—not produce guaranteed cinematic wins. Skill input remains relevant.

## AI

AI personalities should affect decisions. A disciplined team takes fewer Show risks; a theatrical team attempts them when the crowd is hot; an aggressive club pressures bases and collisions.
