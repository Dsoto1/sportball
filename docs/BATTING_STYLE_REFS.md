# Batting Style Reference Prompts

> **Superseded as the authority** by [`BATTING_STYLE_SPEC.md`](BATTING_STYLE_SPEC.md)
> (the owner's revised spec: locked world rules, per-style mechanics, QA
> checklist). The first generated sheet is `batting_styles_reference.jpg`.
> The prompts below are kept for making more images.

Prompts for reference images of the six batting styles in
`presentation/animation/batting_styles.gd`. Each image is one **pose sheet**:
the same batter in five key poses, left to right, so the poses can be read
off and matched in the baked animations.

## How to use them

- Paste the **shared block** first, then one **style block**.
- Generate one sheet per style (six sheets).
- If a sheet comes back wrong, regenerate just that style.
- Lefties are mirrored in code, so right-handed sheets are all we need.

## Shared block (paste before every style)

> Character pose reference sheet for a baseball video game. One right-handed
> baseball batter shown five times in a single row, left to right, as five
> key moments of one swing: 1 STANCE, 2 LOAD, 3 STRIDE, 4 CONTACT, 5 FINISH.
> Label each panel underneath in plain capital letters.
>
> Camera: the same in every panel. Full body, head to cleats in frame, seen
> from the front of home plate looking straight at the batter's chest, so his
> left shoulder points toward the pitcher (screen left) and the catcher is
> behind him (screen right). Eye-level camera, no perspective tilt, no
> cropping.
>
> Figure: athletic adult male in a plain, untextured baseball uniform: solid
> jersey, white pants, high socks, black cleats, batting helmet, batting
> gloves. No logos, no numbers, no text on the clothes. Neutral face. Wooden
> bat. Clean, readable silhouette; realistic proportions and anatomy; both
> hands clearly gripping the bat where the pose calls for it.
>
> Background: plain flat light gray, no field, no crowd, no shadows on the
> background. Soft even studio light. Stylized 3D render look, like a
> character turnaround sheet. Not a real player, no likeness of any real
> person.

## Style blocks

### 1. Classic

> Style: CLASSIC, balanced and textbook.
> 1 STANCE: feet a little wider than the shoulders, knees soft, slight forward
> lean from the hips, weight even. Hands together at the back shoulder,
> about chin height. Bat angled up and back at about 45 degrees over the back
> shoulder. Head turned to look at the pitcher.
> 2 LOAD: weight shifts onto the back leg, hips and shoulders coil slightly
> away from the pitcher, hands drift back a few inches, front heel lifts
> slightly.
> 3 STRIDE: front foot steps a short distance toward the pitcher and lands
> softly on the ball of the foot, hands still back, bat still up: fully
> cocked.
> 4 CONTACT: hips fully open toward the pitcher, back heel up and back foot
> pivoting, arms extending, bat level and square across the front of home
> plate, eyes on the contact point.
> 5 FINISH: chest facing the pitcher, bat wrapped high over the front
> shoulder with both hands still on it, back foot up on its toe.

### 2. Crouch

> Style: CROUCH, deep and wide, a low power base.
> 1 STANCE: very wide feet, deep knee bend, chest leaning well forward over
> the plate, hands low near the back armpit, bat flatter (about 30 degrees),
> eyes level with the strike zone.
> 2 LOAD: sinks a little lower into the back hip, shoulders coil, hands push
> back.
> 3 STRIDE: long low stride toward the pitcher without rising up; stays in
> the crouch.
> 4 CONTACT: stays low through the ball, hips open, bat level and square
> across the plate, back knee driving toward the front.
> 5 FINISH: still lower than a classic finish, bat wrapped over the front
> shoulder, both hands on.

### 3. Open

> Style: OPEN, front foot pulled away from the plate.
> 1 STANCE: front foot set back toward the third-base side so the chest
> half-faces the pitcher; toes of the front foot turned out; hands high
> near the back ear, bat fairly upright, both eyes squarely on the pitcher.
> 2 LOAD: a toe tap: the front foot lifts and swings back in so his feet are
> parallel to the plate, closing him up; shoulders coil.
> 3 STRIDE: short stride straight at the pitcher from the closed position,
> hands back.
> 4 CONTACT: hips open, bat level and square across the plate.
> 5 FINISH: high two-hand finish over the front shoulder.

### 4. Leg Kick

> Style: LEG KICK, big timing kick and long stride, a slugger.
> 1 STANCE: taller and more upright than normal, hands high above the back
> shoulder, bat upright at about 60 degrees.
> 2 LOAD: big leg kick: the front knee lifts high toward the chest (thigh
> near horizontal), balanced on the back leg, hands and bat pull back and up,
> shoulders coiled hard away from the pitcher.
> 3 STRIDE: the front foot drives forward into a long stride and plants
> firmly; hands stay back, the body stretched like a spring.
> 4 CONTACT: explosive hip rotation, back foot pivoting hard, arms
> extending, bat level and square across the plate.
> 5 FINISH: full rotation, chest past the pitcher, bat high and wrapped
> around behind the head.

### 5. Bat Wrap

> Style: BAT WRAP, bat cocked behind the head, a power hitter.
> 1 STANCE: hands high beside the back ear; the bat lies nearly flat and
> points behind his head toward the pitcher (the barrel wraps around behind
> the helmet); back elbow lifted high.
> 2 LOAD: hands push slightly further back and up; the barrel tips a little
> more toward the pitcher behind the head.
> 3 STRIDE: medium stride toward the pitcher, the bat still wrapped.
> 4 CONTACT: the barrel whips around from behind the head, level and square
> across the plate, arms extending.
> 5 FINISH: one-hand release: the top hand lets go and drops to his side,
> the bottom hand carries the bat high over the front shoulder.

### 6. Slap

> Style: SLAP, contact hitter, quick and compact.
> 1 STANCE: narrow stance, slight crouch, hands choked a few inches up the
> handle (gap between the knob and the bottom hand), hands low and out in
> front of the back shoulder, bat at about 45 degrees.
> 2 LOAD: barely loads: a small inward turn of the front knee and a slight
> hands-back.
> 3 STRIDE: a short, quick step toward the pitcher.
> 4 CONTACT: short, direct path, the bat level and square across the plate,
> chest only partly open, already starting to move toward first base.
> 5 FINISH: compact and level: arms extended out in front at chest height,
> the bat pointing toward the pitcher's side, body already leaning toward
> first base, both hands on.

## Bunt sheet (one sheet, front and side)

One sheet with two rows: the bunt from the front (top row) and from behind
the catcher (bottom row). Paste as is.

> Character pose reference sheet for a baseball video game. One right-handed
> baseball batter squaring around to bunt, shown as four key moments in two
> rows. Top row: seen from the front of home plate, looking at the batter's
> chest, his left shoulder toward the pitcher (screen left). Bottom row: the
> same four moments seen from directly behind the catcher, looking out
> toward the pitcher. Label each panel underneath in plain capital letters.
>
> 1 STANCE: a normal balanced batting stance, hands at the back shoulder,
> bat angled up and back.
> 2 SQUARING AROUND: he pivots on the balls of both feet so his chest turns
> to face the pitcher; the back foot steps up level with the front foot;
> the bat starts to come down in front of him.
> 3 BUNT: chest square to the pitcher, feet shoulder-width apart and
> parallel, knees well bent, weight slightly forward. The bat is held flat
> and level out in front of his body at the top of the strike zone, arms
> relaxed and slightly bent. The bottom hand stays near the knob; the top
> hand slides about halfway up the barrel and pinches the bat from behind
> with the fingers safely tucked. Eyes level with the bat.
> 4 CONTACT: same as BUNT, but the bat gives back slightly toward his body as
> if catching the ball softly, the barrel angled a little toward the first
> base side.
>
> Camera: the same distance and height in every panel. Full body, head to
> cleats in frame, eye level, no perspective tilt, no cropping.
>

## Top-down foot placement diagram

The game draws its own exact diagram from the poses
(`docs/batting_feet_topdown.png`; redraw with `tools/foot_diagram_data.gd`
and `tools/draw_foot_diagram.py`). Use this prompt for a hand-made or
AI-drawn target to compare it with. Per the spec, the diagram wins over any
artistic reference on foot placement.

> Technical top-down diagram, flat vector style like a coaching manual, on a
> white background, for six right-handed baseball batting styles in a 3 x 2
> grid: 1 CLASSIC, 2 CROUCH, 3 OPEN, 4 LEG KICK, 5 BAT WRAP, 6 SLAP. Each
> panel is the same view straight down from above, the same scale, the
> pitcher at the top of the panel and the catcher at the bottom.
>
> In every panel: home plate drawn to scale (17 inches across, the point
> toward the catcher) on the right; the right-handed batter's box to its left
> (4 feet wide by 6 feet long, its inner line 6 inches from the plate's
> edge), drawn as a thin gray outline; an arrow at the top labelled "to the
> pitcher". Nothing else: no player, no bat, no field, no shadows.
>
> Show the batter's two feet as simple footprint shapes (heel and toe
> clearly different), toes pointing toward the plate at the stance, in five
> colors with a legend: STANCE gray, LOAD blue, FOOT PLANT orange, CONTACT
> red, FINISH green. A dashed outline means the foot is off the ground or up
> on its toe. Draw an arrow for the lead (left) foot's travel from STANCE to
> FOOT PLANT, and a curved arrow for the rear (right) foot's pivot from
> FOOT PLANT to FINISH.
>
> Rules for every style: both feet stay inside the batter's box; no foot
> touches the plate; the rear foot stays put through LOAD, then pivots on
> the ball of the foot (heel turning toward the catcher) through CONTACT and
> FINISH; the lead foot strides toward the pitcher (straight up the panel),
> lands about 30 degrees open toward the pitcher, and stays planted through
> CONTACT and FINISH.
>
> Per style:
> 1 CLASSIC: feet a little wider than the shoulders, parallel to the plate's
> edge; a short stride (about 7 inches) straight toward the pitcher.
> 2 CROUCH: a much wider stance; a short, low stride (about 7 inches) to a
> very wide base.
> 3 OPEN: the lead foot starts pulled back away from the plate with its toes
> turned toward the pitcher; at LOAD a toe tap brings it back in, level with
> the rear foot; then a short stride toward the pitcher.
> 4 LEG KICK: stance like CLASSIC; at LOAD the lead foot is lifted high in
> the air (dashed) and drawn back near the rear foot; then the longest
> stride of the six (about 12 inches) toward the pitcher.
> 5 BAT WRAP: feet like CLASSIC, a medium stride.
> 6 SLAP: a narrow stance; the shortest stride (about 5 inches), already
> leaning toward first base at FINISH.
>
> Label each panel with its style name in bold at the top left. Clean
> lines, readable labels, no decorative elements.
