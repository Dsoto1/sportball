# SHOW & PERSONALITY SYSTEMS

## Score vs. Show

Every match tracks conventional baseball score and a separate audience response state. Score wins games. Show grows spectacle. Show should never secretly replace the scoreboard.

## Show Sources

Great baseball can generate Show: home runs, strikeouts, diving catches, steals, double plays. Intentional entertainment can generate more: trick plays, dramatic celebrations, daring collisions, rivalry moments, crowd interactions.

## Risk/Reward

Routine action: high reliability, normal baseball reward.

Show variant: reduced reliability or increased consequence, higher audience reward.

This trade is the heart of the system.

## Crowd State

Use a readable state such as Cold -> Engaged -> Loud -> Wild -> Electric. Crowd state may influence audiovisual intensity, confidence, sponsor objectives, fame, and certain traits. Avoid large invisible stat bonuses that make baseball outcomes feel arbitrary.

## Core Player Attributes

Contact, Power, Speed, Fielding, Arm, Pitching attributes as needed, Toughness, Charisma. Charisma affects audience conversion and off-field value rather than raw batting power.

## Traits

Traits create exceptions and personality: Brick Wall, Showboat, Hot Head, Fan Favorite, Clutch, Cannon Arm, Bad Knees, Ice Cold, Team Leader, Rival Hunter. Keep traits legible and situational.

## Rivalries

Track player-player and team-team rivalry intensity from meaningful events: hit-by-pitch, repeated strikeouts, collisions, postseason meetings, taunts, dramatic reversals. Rivalries alter presentation, event generation, crowd interest, and limited situational modifiers.

## Celebrations

Unlockable solo, duo, and team routines. They are primarily identity/reward content, with limited systemic impact. Avoid forcing long celebrations after every event.

## Failure Is Content

A failed trick should often be funny, costly, and memorable—not merely a red X. Animation, commentary, crowd reaction, and rivalry consequences turn failure into story.
