# ART DIRECTION

## Style Statement

Stylized retro-futurist sports anime with 1990s arcade exaggeration. The game should feel like a lost 1990s arcade baseball franchise reinvented as a modern 3D title.

## Character Proportions

Athletic but exaggerated, roughly 5-6 heads tall. Larger hands, gloves, shoulders, shoes, equipment, and facial features improve silhouette/readability. Power hitters feel massive; speedsters feel spring-loaded; catchers feel armored; finesse pitchers can be lanky. Do not use chibi proportions.

## Rendering

Stylized 3D gameplay with illustrated sensibility: clean forms, selective detail, bold readable materials, strong silhouettes, deliberate rim/key lighting, expressive faces, and controlled impact effects. Avoid photoreal skin/materials and over-detailed PBR noise.

## Animation Language

Baseball fundamentals must read correctly, then exaggerate anticipation, impact, follow-through, recoil, celebration, and recovery. Use tasteful squash/stretch and camera punch for arcade impact. Characters should remain responsive; spectacle cannot lock controls unnecessarily.

## Retro-Future Technology

Chunky mechanical scoreboards, exposed actuators, reinforced gear, CRT/LED-inspired displays, physical switches, colorful armor, industrial stadium hardware. Technology should look designed for sport, not imported from generic cyberpunk.

## Stadiums

Baseball park + concert venue + amusement park. Each stadium needs a unique silhouette, crowd culture, scoreboard language, attractions, lighting personality, and gameplay identity. Small clubs should look charmingly improvised; elite venues can become outrageous spectacles.

## Crowds

Dense visual storytelling: signs, chants, coordinated sections, mascots, bands, vendors, costumes, reactions, fan catches, celebrations. Use LOD/crowd batching for performance; perceived variety matters more than unique high-detail spectators.

## 2D Portraits

Illustrated sports-manga/trading-card portraits for roster, events, rivalry screens, and career milestones. Portraits may evolve by career tier while preserving recognizable identity.

## Graphic Design

Sports broadcast crossed with arcade cabinet: large scores, clear base state, bold player portraits, team marks, punchy event callouts, readable typography, and limited retro-future ornamentation.

## Color

Team identity should dominate. Stadium and UI palettes support teams rather than washing everything in universal neon. Neon is an accent, not the art direction.

## Do Not Drift Toward

- Photoreal MLB presentation
- Chibi characters
- Fortnite/hero-shooter visual language
- Generic mobile 3D
- Dark dystopian cyberpunk
- Constant neon holograms
- Anime visual noise that harms sports readability
- Real-world team/player likenesses
